# HACKATHON SUBMISSION

## Mission

Transform the completed hackathon product, demo, visual system, and technical
proof into a complete, credible, judge-aligned submission package.

The Submission Department must ensure that every required artifact is:

- complete;
- accurate;
- easy to understand;
- aligned with judging criteria;
- supported by evidence;
- consistent with the demo and repository;
- consistent with the approved distinction;
- visually coherent;
- truthful about evidence state.

The objective is not to make the project sound bigger.

The objective is to make the project easy to evaluate, difficult to
misunderstand, and easy to remember.

---

- Claim Auditor
- Submission Compliance Analyst

- evidence labels that change between artifacts;
- unreviewed Antigravity outputs;
- post-freeze submission drift.

# Core Principles

## Principle 1 — Submission Must Match Reality

Every statement must reflect:

- what works;
- what is demonstrated;
- what is deployed;
- what is experimental;
- what is simulated;
- what is pre-seeded;
- what remains future work.

Never upgrade evidence quality through copy.

---

## Principle 2 — Judges Read Selectively

Put the strongest information early:

1. problem;
2. product action;
3. Signature Behavior;
4. visible proof;
5. consequence;
6. differentiation;
7. sponsor causality;
8. technical depth;
9. limitations;
10. next step.

---

## Principle 3 — Evidence Must Be Traceable

Every important claim should map to:

- demo evidence;
- repository evidence;
- data evidence;
- architecture evidence;
- source evidence;
- visual evidence;
- evidence label.

Claims without a trace must be removed, softened, or reframed.

---

## Principle 4 — Technical Depth Should Be Legible

Explain the technical contribution clearly without overwhelming non-technical
judges.

Technical depth should appear as:

- visible behavior;
- proof object;
- causal architecture;
- verification path;
- reliable fallback;
- reproducible evidence.

---

## Principle 5 — Sponsor Technology Must Be Causal

State:

- where it is used;
- why it is necessary;
- what capability it enables;
- what would fail or materially weaken without it;
- how judges can verify it;
- where it becomes visible in the demo or product.

Do not treat sponsor technology as a stack badge.

---

## Principle 6 — Distinction Must Stay Consistent

The submission must preserve:

- one Judge Memory Sentence;
- one Signature Behavior;
- one Signature Moment;
- one Product Metaphor when used;
- one primary chronological story;
- one head-to-head advantage.

Do not invent a second project identity in the README, video, or submission text.

---

## Principle 7 — Visual Assets Are Evidence-Carrying Artifacts

Screenshots and video frames must:

- match the working product;
- show the strongest state;
- expose visible proof;
- preserve evidence labels;
- reinforce the Judge Memory Sentence;
- avoid debug clutter;
- avoid empty setup states.

Visual polish supports truth. It does not replace truth.

---

## Principle 8 — Submission Is a Scoring Interface

Each submission section should help a judge score at least one:

- relevance;
- innovation;
- technical implementation;
- sponsor usage;
- completeness;
- impact;
- usability;
- reliability.

Remove sections that do not help evaluation or compliance.

State:

- primary user;
- pain;
- consequence;
- current limitation;
- why the current alternative fails.

---

```text

↓

↓
Signature Behavior
↓
Visible Proof
↓
Consequence
↓
User Action
↓
Outcome
```

The description must be understandable without architecture knowledge.

---

## Signature Behavior

State:

- what real condition triggers it;
- what visible state changes;
- what proof appears;
- what consequence follows;
- why a generic alternative cannot produce the same judge experience.

Do not describe the Signature Behavior as marketing copy only.

---

## How It Works

Explain:

- data flow;
- model or agent role;
- tools;
- sponsor technology;
- result generation;
- verification;
- fallback;
- evidence classification.

---

## Visible Proof

Explain:

- the central claim;
- the proof artifact;
- where the judge sees it;
- how it can be verified;
- the evidence label;
- any limitation.

---

## Challenges

Include real problems:

- technical failures;
- integration issues;
- latency;
- data limitations;
- UX tradeoffs;
- visual implementation constraints;
- browser issues;
- time constraints;
- fallback design.

Do not invent dramatic challenges.

---

## Accomplishments

Focus on:

- working end-to-end workflow;
- deployed system;
- Signature Behavior;
- visible technical proof;
- sponsor-causal mechanism;
- reliability;
- user value;
- demo evidence;
- browser or test evidence.

---

## What We Learned

Explain:

- technical insight;
- product insight;
- distinction insight;
- UX insight;
- visual-production insight;
- judging insight;
- limitation discovered.

---

## Limitations

State:

- data limitations;
- reliability limitations;
- model limitations;
- evidence limitations;
- production gaps;
- browser or device constraints;
- regulatory limits;
- simulated or pre-seeded components.

Honest limitations increase credibility.

---

## What's Next

Separate:

- immediate technical next step;
- missing proof;
- real-world validation;
- product expansion;
- commercialization.

Do not describe future work as already delivered.

# Token Efficiency Rules

1. **Reuse Existing Artifacts**  
   Do not rewrite Product, Distinction, UX, Visual Production, or Demo analysis.

2. **Write to Platform Requirements**  
   Do not produce unused sections.

3. **Use Claim Mapping**  
   Avoid repetitive proof explanations.

4. **Patch Weak Sections**  
   Do not regenerate the full submission.

5. **Limit Technical Detail**  
   Include only what judges need to verify.

6. **Avoid Duplicate Narratives**  
   README, submission, and video may share facts, but not duplicate full prose.

7. **Use One Distinction Language**  
   Preserve the same Judge Memory Sentence and Signature Behavior.

8. **Reference Visual Packets**  
   Do not redesign screenshots inside Submission.

9. **Freeze After Verification**  
   Do not keep polishing once links, claims, and assets pass.

# Submission Failure Modes

## Overclaiming

Failure:

Claims exceed the demo or repository.

Fix:

Map claims to evidence and downgrade language.

---

## Generic Description

Failure:

Copy could describe any AI project.

Fix:

Use the specific trigger, Signature Behavior, proof, consequence, and sponsor
causality.

---

## Distinction Drift

Failure:

README, video, and submission describe different reasons the project is unique.

Fix:

Restore the approved Judge Memory Sentence and Signature Behavior.

---

## Architecture Overload

Failure:

Technical detail obscures value.

Fix:

Lead with problem, product behavior, proof, and consequence.

---

## Sponsor Technology Hidden

Failure:

Judges cannot see sponsor relevance.

Fix:

Create an explicit causal section and visible proof.

---

## Sponsor Logo as Proof

Failure:

The sponsor is visible only as branding.

Fix:

Show what the sponsor technology enables and what changes without it.

---

## Missing Limitations

Failure:

Project sounds unrealistic.

Fix:

Disclose constraints and evidence boundaries.

---

## Broken Links

Failure:

Judges cannot verify the project.

Fix:

Test all links and permissions.

---

## Inconsistent Terminology

Failure:

Name, workflow, features, and states differ across artifacts.

Fix:

Run the full consistency check.

---

## Screenshot Mismatch

Failure:

Screenshots show a state not available in the submitted build.

Fix:

Recapture from the frozen version or disclose the difference.

---

## Polished Evidence Inflation

Failure:

Visual polish makes simulated or pre-seeded evidence appear live.

Fix:

Restore visible labels and correct captions.

---

## Unreviewed Antigravity Asset

Failure:

A screenshot or browser artifact is used without Claude audit.

Fix:

Audit or remove the artifact.

Copy, screenshots, or repository change after final verification.

Fix:

Reopen explicitly, record the reason, and rerun affected checks.

# Final Rule

The Submission Department should make the project easy to score, verify, and
remember.

It must answer:

- What is the project?
- Who is it for?
- What problem does it solve?
- What does it do?
- What is the Signature Behavior?
- Where is the Signature Moment?
- What proves it?
- Why is sponsor technology causally necessary?
- Why is it stronger than the competent generic baseline?
- What are the limitations?
- What should judges inspect?
- Which evidence labels apply?
- Do README, video, screenshots, repository, and live product describe the same
  thing?
- Is the package frozen and safe to submit?

If those answers are unclear, the submission is not ready.
