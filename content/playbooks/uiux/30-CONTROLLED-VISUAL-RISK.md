# 30 — Controlled Visual Risk

Purpose: make the agent propose more distinctive visual ideas without sacrificing clarity, proof, trust, or mobile readability.

Core line: clarity is mandatory. Blandness is not.

## Use when

- The design feels safe, generic, or easily replaceable.
- The project needs a memorable visual moment.
- A landing page, portfolio page, client site, dashboard, or demo needs stronger identity.
- The UI is clear but lacks character.

## Risk budget

Choose 1–3 areas of boldness only:
- typography;
- color;
- buttons/CTA;
- iconography;
- composition;
- motion;
- interaction libraries;
- 3D/WebGL.

Do not make all areas experimental at once.

## Risk levels

Always consider three options when the project needs visual direction:

1. Safe — clear, restrained, low risk.
2. Bold — stronger typography, CTA, palette, or layout while preserving clarity.
3. Signature — one memorable visual idea tied to the proof or product moment.


## Typography risk

Examples:
- distinctive hero font;
- serif + sans pairing;
- grotesk + condensed pairing;
- controlled monospace for proof fields;
- stronger type scale;
- editorial headline rhythm.

Use Fontshare or project-specific type references.

## Color risk

Examples:
- less expected accent;
- stronger primary/accent relationship;
- distinct status palette;
- expressive but readable background system.

Palette mixing rule:
- primary and accent colors must work together;
- CTA remains visually dominant;
- status labels remain meaningful;
- text contrast stays readable;
- proof cards remain readable;
- palette scales across hero, cards, dashboard, and mobile.

## Button / CTA risk

Examples:
- icon + text CTA;
- stronger hover state;
- gradient button when it supports hierarchy;
- split CTA;
- more distinctive radius/border/shadow system.

Use Gradient Buttons by Colorion only when the CTA benefits from more personality.

## Iconography risk

Use Reicon, Nucleo, Central Icons, 123Done, or Untitled UI Icons.

Specify icon set, stroke weight, style, size scale, and usage zones.

## Motion / interaction risk

Use Kinetics, Ripplix, NumberFlow, Liveline, cmdk, dnd kit, Virtuoso, Leva, input-otp, Coverflow, or threecn only when they improve a product moment.

Do not animate critical evidence labels or limitations.

## 3D / WebGL risk

Use threecn or similar only if:
- it communicates the product idea;
- it improves understanding or memory;
- it still works in screenshots/video;
- there is a static/mobile fallback;
- performance risk is acceptable.

## Hard limits

Controlled visual risk must never:
- reduce proof readability;
- weaken evidence labels;
- hide limitations;
- make stubbed/simulated/projected work feel live;
- damage mobile usability;
- create accessibility failure;
- overpower the main product result.
