# 31 — UX Research Methods

Purpose: help the agent choose lightweight UX research and validation methods instead of making design decisions only by taste.

## Research routing

Before major UX decisions, identify the research need:

- Need to understand users/problems → generative research.
- Need to validate a design/prototype → evaluative research.
- Need to understand opinions/motivations → attitudinal research.
- Need to prove actual behavior → behavioral research.
- Need depth/context → qualitative research.
- Need scale/comparison → quantitative research.

Do not over-research small projects. Use the lightest method that can produce a useful decision.

## Method picker

Use interviews when:
- the problem is unclear;
- motivation matters;
- language, objections, and trust concerns matter.

Use surveys when:
- broader directional input is needed;
- preferences or friction need quantification.

Use card sorting when:
- navigation, categories, or service grouping is unclear.

Use usability testing when:
- users need to understand or complete a flow;
- CTA clarity or task completion matters.

Use A/B testing when:
- two versions are ready;
- there is enough traffic or test volume.

Use analytics/heatmaps/session recordings when:
- an existing site has behavioral data;
- click paths, scroll depth, or attention need validation.

Use co-design/brainstorming when:
- stakeholder/user ideas need to shape the solution early.

## Lightweight research for fast projects

For small client, hackathon, or portfolio projects, prefer:
- 3 user/stakeholder questions;
- 1 stakeholder interview;
- 1 mobile usability check;
- 1 before/after friction map;
- 1 measurable behavior to track later.

## Evidence principle

Behavioral evidence is stronger than claimed opinions when proving UX.

Examples:
- “CTA was visible above the fold on mobile.”
- “Booking path reduced from 4 steps to 2.”
- “User completed the test flow without explanation.”
- “Result card was understood in 5 seconds.”

Never claim business outcomes unless measured. Use PROJECTED for expected outcomes until real evidence exists.
