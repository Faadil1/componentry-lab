## Fixture A: Sustainable Skincare
* **Subject**: Skincare Campaign A
* **Category**: skincare
* **Objective**: create a distinctive premium campaign
* **Audience**: environmentally conscious skincare buyers
* **Desired Position**: active and culturally relevant rather than quiet naturalism
* **Evaluator**: creative director
* **Trust Requirements**: efficacy, safety, environmental credibility

### 1. Conventions Discovered
1. **Clinical Efficacy Frame** (`clinical-efficacy-frame`)
   * Statement: Efficacy claims must be clinically supported or implied through lab-adjacent aesthetic (white space, scientific language, before/after imagery)
   * Why: Safety-first regulatory environment and consumer trust deficit in beauty claims.
2. **Wellness Tone** (`wellness-tone`)
   * Statement: Brand tone is gentle, aspirational, and wellness-adjacent — never confrontational or urgent
   * Why: Anxiety-reduction is the primary purchase emotion in personal care.
3. **Aspirational Lifestyle Photography** (`aspirational-lifestyle-photography`)
   * Statement: Photography depicts aspirational but accessible skin — conventionally attractive models in soft achievable contexts
   * Why: Social comparison drives category desire.
4. **Benefit Storytelling over Identity** (`benefit-storytelling-over-identity`)
   * Statement: Storytelling centres product benefits and personal transformation — cultural or political identity is avoided
   * Why: Broad audience appeal: political/cultural positioning risks alienating mainstream buyers.
5. **Sustainability Third-Party Validation** (`sustainability-third-party-validation`)
   * Statement: Sustainability claims require visible certification logos or third-party validation
   * Why: Greenwashing skepticism has trained buyers to require proof.

### 2. Sacred vs. Habit Classification
* **SACRED**: `clinical-efficacy-frame` (Protected by trust requirement: "efficacy")
* **SACRED**: `sustainability-third-party-validation` (Protected by trust requirement: "environmental credibility")
* **HABIT**: `wellness-tone` (Challenged by desired position "active" -> strength: 1)
* **HABIT**: `aspirational-lifestyle-photography` (Challenged by desired position "culturally relevant" -> strength: 1)
* **HABIT**: `benefit-storytelling-over-identity` (Challenged by desired position "culturally relevant" -> strength: 1)

### 3. Selected Strategic Inversion
* **Target Habit**: `wellness-tone`
* **Strategic Value**: Aligned with desired position "active" (signals: [active] oppose this convention).
* **Strategic Inversion Synthesis**:
  > The convention "Brand tone is gentle, aspirational, and wellness-adjacent — never confrontational or urgent" is directly opposed by the desired position "active and culturally relevant rather than quiet naturalism". Replace soft wellness aesthetics with active, high-impact design language that signals urgency and forward movement.
* **Preserved Category Signals**:
  * Clinical Efficacy Frame (preserved for efficacy/safety trust)
  * Sustainability Third-Party Validation (preserved for environmental credibility trust)
* **Implications**:
  * **Visual**: Abandon soft lighting and muted wellness tones. Use high-impact typography, sharp layouts, and bold color accents.
  * **Verbal**: Replace passive wellness language ("nourish", "gentle") with active, direct claims ("active recovery", "verifiable impact").
  * **Motion**: Fast-paced edits, energetic transitions, and sharp reveals rather than slow fades.
  * **Failure Risks**: Wellness tone disruption risks looking sterile or aggressive; must be anchored by strong efficacy validation.
* **Proposed Validation**: Show the work to environmentally conscious skincare buyers for 5 seconds. Ask: "What position does this occupy?" The answer must include words like "active", "urgent", or "culturally engaged", not standard calm wellness descriptions.

## Fixture B: B2B SaaS Analytics Dashboard
* **Subject**: Dashboard B
* **Category**: saas
* **Objective**: differentiate from generic enterprise dashboards
* **Audience**: operations leaders
* **Desired Position**: decisive and operational rather than passive reporting
* **Evaluator**: product stakeholder
* **Trust Requirements**: clarity, credibility, legibility

### 1. Conventions Discovered
1. **Data Density Signals Capability** (`data-density-signals-capability`)
   * Statement: Dashboards default to dense data visualization — visible data complexity signals analytical depth
   * Why: Enterprise buyers equate visible data complexity with analytical capability.
2. **Enterprise Visual Restraint** (`enterprise-visual-restraint`)
   * Statement: Enterprise design communicates seriousness through visual restraint, muted palette, and conservative structure
   * Why: Risk-averse procurement culture selects for safe, institutional aesthetics.
3. **Feature Comparison Transparency** (`feature-comparison-transparency`)
   * Statement: Feature comparison tables signal buying transparency and competitive confidence
   * Why: B2B buyers must justify decisions to procurement and stakeholders.
4. **Social Proof Brand Logos** (`social-proof-brand-logos`)
   * Statement: Credibility requires recognized enterprise brand logos and named testimonials
   * Why: B2B trust transfers through peer institution credibility.
5. **Complexity Signals Power** (`complexity-signals-power`)
   * Statement: Interface complexity signals analytical power — simple interfaces appear under-featured
   * Why: Legacy enterprise tooling conditioned buyers to associate complexity with power.

### 2. Sacred vs. Habit Classification
* **SACRED**: `feature-comparison-transparency` (Protected by trust requirement: "credibility")
* **HABIT**: `data-density-signals-capability` (Challenged by desired position "decisive", "operational" -> strength: 2)
* **HABIT**: `enterprise-visual-restraint` (Challenged by desired position "decisive", "operational" -> strength: 2)
* **HABIT**: `complexity-signals-power` (Challenged by desired position "decisive", "operational" -> strength: 2)
* **HABIT**: `social-proof-brand-logos` (Challenged by desired position "decisive", "operational" -> strength: 2)

### 3. Selected Strategic Inversion
* **Target Habit**: `data-density-signals-capability`
* **Strategic Value**: Position-driven (strength 2). Directly opposes passive reporting.
* **Strategic Inversion Synthesis**:
  > The convention "Dashboards default to dense data visualization — visible data complexity signals analytical depth" is directly opposed by the desired position "decisive and operational rather than passive reporting". Invert this by focusing on single, high-leverage decision paths and outcome indicators rather than grid-locked charts.
* **Preserved Category Signals**:
  * Feature Comparison Transparency (preserved for credibility trust)
* **Implications**:
  * **Visual**: Shift from dense chart tables to large, single-focus operational decision blocks.
  * **Verbal**: Replace passive reporting labels with action-oriented headers ("Immediate Action Needed", "Risk Resolved").
  * **Motion**: Transition quickly and cleanly to show operational state shifts instantly.
  * **Failure Risks**: Simplicity reads as "basic" or underpowered without a strong operational narrative reframe.
* **Proposed Validation**: Show the dashboard to operations leaders. Ask: "Is this tool for monitoring or for acting?" If they answer "monitoring", the strategic inversion has failed.

## Fixture C: Premium Home Renovation Service
* **Subject**: Renovation C
* **Category**: renovation
* **Objective**: make expertise and accountability feel tangible
* **Audience**: homeowners
* **Desired Position**: premium, human, high-trust
* **Evaluator**: prospective client
* **Trust Requirements**: reliability, competence, price confidence

### 1. Conventions Discovered
1. **Before-After Portfolio** (`before-after-portfolio`)
   * Statement: Portfolio photography shows dramatic before/after transformations as the primary proof of capability
   * Why: Visual transformation proof reduces purchase uncertainty in irreversible, high-cost decisions.
2. **Certification & Years in Business** (`certification-years-in-business`)
   * Statement: Trust is established through certification badges and years-in-business claims
   * Why: Risk reduction shortcuts for competence in unfamiliar domains.
3. **Price Opacity** (`price-opacity`)
   * Statement: Pricing transparency is avoided until prospect qualification to allow premium anchoring
   * Why: High-ticket services use scarcity and personalization framing to justify price.
4. **Craftsmanship Artisan Language** (`craftsmanship-artisan-language`)
   * Statement: Brand identity centres on craftsmanship, artisanship, and trade expertise
   * Why: Quality differentiation in a commoditized market.
5. **Explicit Guarantee Prominence** (`explicit-guarantee-prominence`)
   * Statement: Service guarantees must be explicit, specific, and featured prominently
   * Why: Irreversible purchase anxiety requires contractual assurance.

### 2. Sacred vs. Habit Classification
* **SACRED**: `certification-years-in-business` (Protected by trust requirement: "reliability")
* **SACRED**: `explicit-guarantee-prominence` (Protected by trust requirement: "competence")
* **SACRED**: `price-opacity` (Protected by trust requirement: "price confidence")
* **HABIT**: `before-after-portfolio` (Challenged by desired position "human", "high-trust" -> strength: 2)
* **HABIT**: `craftsmanship-artisan-language` (Challenged by desired position "human" -> strength: 1)

### 3. Selected Strategic Inversion
* **Target Habit**: `before-after-portfolio`
* **Strategic Inversion Synthesis**:
  > The convention "Portfolio photography shows dramatic before/after transformations as the primary proof of capability" is directly challenged by the desired position "premium, human, high-trust". Instead of showing empty completed rooms, show the human construction process, accountability benchmarks, and craftsman interaction details to establish a relationship-driven premium.
* **Preserved Category Signals**:
  * Certification & Years in Business
  * Explicit Guarantee Prominence
  * Price Opacity
* **Implications**:
  * **Visual**: Replace sterile before/after split-screens with documentary-style imagery showing human builders, specific construction details, and material audits.
  * **Verbal**: Highlight client-builder communications, process updates, and transparency values rather than pure output adjectives.
  * **Motion**: Smooth, slower pacing reflecting careful workmanship and transparency.
  * **Failure Risks**: Removing before/after images risks reducing visual evidence of capacity; must be replaced by high-end process documentation.
* **Proposed Validation**: Show the portfolio page to prospective clients. If they describe it as a "typical construction site" rather than a "highly structured, collaborative service", the inversion has not succeeded.
